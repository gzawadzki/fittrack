# Training volume landmarks (MEV, MAV, MRV)

Volume landmarks describe a range of weekly training volume (typically counted in hard sets
per muscle group) that produce different outcomes for a given individual:

- MV (maintenance volume): the minimum volume that maintains current adaptations.
- MEV (minimum effective volume): the least volume that still produces meaningful progress.
- MAV (maximum adaptive volume): the volume range that produces the best rate of progress.
- MRV (maximum recoverable volume): the ceiling beyond which added volume degrades recovery
  faster than it contributes to adaptation.

Key points:

- These landmarks are individual and shift over time with training age, recovery capacity,
  and life stress — they are not fixed numbers that transfer directly between athletes.
- Volume increases should be gradual (e.g. one set at a time per muscle group) so that
  the athlete's response can be observed before committing to a larger change.
- When recovery markers (sleep, resting heart rate) suggest accumulated fatigue, trimming
  volume by a set or two on affected movements is generally a safer first intervention than
  reducing load, since it preserves the movement pattern and intensity while reducing total
  stress.
- Step count and general daily activity provide useful context for total weekly stress but
  should not, on their own, be treated as sufficient justification for changing a training
  plan — they are context, not a standalone trigger.
