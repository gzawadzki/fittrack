# Sleep, resting heart rate, and training readiness

Sleep duration/quality and resting heart rate (RHR) are two of the more accessible,
wearable-derived indicators of recovery status.

Key points:

- Meaningful comparisons require an individual baseline built from at least ~7-14 days of
  history; a single day's reading in isolation is not reliably interpretable.
- Sleep duration noticeably below an individual's own recent median (not a population
  average) is a more useful signal than an absolute number of hours.
- An RHR elevated several beats per minute above an individual's recent baseline can indicate
  incomplete recovery, illness onset, or accumulated fatigue; it is a signal to interpret
  cautiously, not a diagnosis.
- When both sleep and RHR are simultaneously off-baseline, the combined signal is stronger
  and more actionable than either alone.
- Readiness signals should inform *pacing* (e.g. holding load, trimming volume) — they are
  context for a training decision, not standalone justification for changing a plan without
  the athlete's confirmation, and they are never a basis for a medical diagnosis.
