# Reps in reserve (RIR) and autoregulated training

Reps in reserve (RIR) is a self-reported estimate of how many more repetitions a lifter
could have performed before technical failure. It is closely related to rating of perceived
exertion (RPE), where RPE 10 corresponds to 0 RIR.

Key points:

- RIR-based autoregulation lets training load adjust to daily readiness instead of following
  a fixed percentage-of-1RM table, which can under- or over-shoot true effort on a given day.
- A target RIR (e.g. "2 RIR on working sets") should be treated as a proxy for effort, not a
  precise physiological measurement — novice lifters in particular tend to underestimate RIR
  (they report more reserve than they actually have).
- Progression decisions that rely on RIR should require the RIR target to be met on *all*
  working sets, not just the best set, to avoid rewarding an outlier performance.
- When RIR is not collected for a given set or session, fall back to a more conservative,
  consistency-based rule (e.g. requiring repeated successful sessions) rather than assuming
  sufficient reserve.
