# Distinguishing delayed-onset soreness from injury signals

Delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS) is a normal, expected response to unfamiliar or
increased training stress. It is distinct from pain that signals injury or a technique
problem, though the two can be difficult to tell apart without a clinical assessment.

Key points:

- DOMS typically: builds over 24-72 hours after a session, is diffuse across a muscle belly,
  eases with light movement/warm-up, and improves day over day.
- Injury/technique-related pain more often: is sharp, localized to a joint or a single point,
  appears suddenly during a specific movement or rep, worsens with continued loading, or is
  accompanied by clicking, instability, numbness, or swelling.
- A coaching application must never attempt to diagnose the cause of pain or a concerning
  symptom. The correct response is to stop progression on the affected exercise, surface the
  signal clearly, and recommend the athlete assess the situation (and see a qualified
  professional if pain persists or worsens) rather than offering a medical opinion.
- Any report of pain, or another concerning symptom (e.g. dizziness, chest tightness,
  shortness of breath disproportionate to effort) during a session should block load or
  volume progression on the affected work, regardless of how good performance otherwise
  looked in that session.
- Technique breakdown reported by the athlete (even without pain) should hold progression at
  the current load until technique is confirmed stable, rather than continuing to add load on
  an unstable movement pattern.
