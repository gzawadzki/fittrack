# Progressive overload and strength adaptation

Progressive overload is the gradual increase of stress placed on the body during training.
Strength and hypertrophy adaptations require a training stimulus that exceeds what the
body has already adapted to.

Key points:

- Load increases should be small and consistent (typically the smallest available plate
  increment, e.g. 2.5 kg) rather than large jumps, to avoid technique breakdown and injury risk.
- Progression should be earned by performance: hitting the top of a prescribed rep range with
  the prescribed effort (reps in reserve) across all working sets is the standard signal to add
  load, not simply "it's been a while."
- When no RIR/effort data is available, two consecutive successful sessions at the same
  load/rep target is a reasonable, more conservative substitute signal before increasing load.
- Missing the bottom of a rep range once is normal session-to-session variation and should not
  immediately trigger a load decrease; missing it in two consecutive sessions is a stronger
  signal that the current load is not yet sustainable.
- Overload can also be applied via added sets (volume) or reduced rest, not only added weight.
